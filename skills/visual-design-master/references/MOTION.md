# Motion Design

## Motion has a job

Every motion should do at least one:
- direct attention;
- reveal hierarchy;
- show cause/effect;
- communicate state change;
- preserve spatial continuity;
- create timing for a joke/payoff.

If it does none, remove it.

## Rhythm grammar

Prefer a readable rhythm:

**hold → action → settle → travel → hold**

Not every beat needs all five.

## Three motion layers

- **Primary motion** — the main story/action.
- **Secondary motion** — reacts to the primary after a small delay.
- **Ambient motion** — subtle state/life; never competes.

Only one layer should dominate at a time.

## Camera

Use camera travel only when spatial relationship matters.
A slow zoom is not automatically cinematic.

Good camera reasons:
- inspect a detail;
- reveal context;
- move to the consequence;
- connect two objects in one world.

## Timing

Fast motion needs a clean hold afterward.
Important information needs time fully visible *after* its entrance animation completes.

## Easing

Match easing to intent:
- decisive = fast ease-out;
- physical = spring with controlled overshoot;
- calm/editorial = smooth cubic;
- mechanical = restrained linear/stepped when conceptually justified.

Do not mix many easing personalities in one short.

## Ending

The final frame should settle. Avoid cutting during unresolved movement unless the loop is intentional.
