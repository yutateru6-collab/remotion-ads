# Typography — Japanese & Mixed Text

## Principle

Typography is a spatial system. Do not “put text on top”; compose with it.

## Roles

Use a small role set:
- Display / impact;
- Headline;
- Body / explanation;
- Label / metadata;
- Numeric / timer if needed.

Do not invent a new style for every sentence.

## Japanese line breaking

- Break by meaning, not by raw character count.
- Keep particles with the phrase they belong to when possible.
- Avoid leaving punctuation alone at the beginning of a line.
- Avoid a one-character orphan line.
- Do not split a punchline across lines unless the split creates the joke.
- For mixed Japanese/English, treat the English phrase as one visual unit when possible.

## Hierarchy

Use weight/size contrast intentionally:
- one strongest phrase;
- supporting text visibly quieter;
- metadata must not shout.

Bold is not a substitute for hierarchy.

## Width and rhythm

Large Japanese display text often looks stronger with fewer characters per line and intentional white space.
Body text needs enough line height to keep dense kanji from becoming a gray block.

## Numerals

Timers, scores and metrics benefit from tabular numerals when they change over time.

## Avoid

- all lines centered by default;
- excessive outline text;
- many unrelated font families;
- random color emphasis on several words;
- shrinking text just to make an overfull frame fit.

Edit the copy or composition before shrinking it into illegibility.
